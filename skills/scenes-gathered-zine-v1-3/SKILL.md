# 实景拼贴 · Gathered Scenes Zine v1.3

作者 / Author: Zeejay0

Modified edition by Shirehuhu (2026-10-14; shared 2026-10-15). Original copyright, source and attribution: see NOTICE. Governed by the unchanged upstream Personal Non-Commercial License in LICENSE. This is not an official upstream release.

Preserve the signature: truthful photography anchors the scene; simplified illustration creates a larger field; one added hue carries structure; torn fibers mark the material handoff; blank paper breathes.

## Source and hierarchy

Inspect the supplied image before composing. Identify the core 1–2 subjects, 2–3 useful supporting elements, essential spatial relationships, dominant gesture, visual-weight distribution, native color atmosphere, natural quiet areas, and 1–2 source shapes that can organize both illustration and color.

Priority: complete principal subjects and scene identity → truthful photographic detail → simplified source-derived illustration → structural color → quiet space → tactile boundary → subordinate text. Preserve people, pose, clothing, props, recognizable details, perspective and essential relationships. Allocation ranges never justify cutting away a principal subject. Naturally correct color before treatment when requested; do not repaint the photographic subject or invent scenery.

## Abstraction

Use medium abstraction: retain the dominant gesture and 1–2 defining forms/relationships, remove about 60–80% of small descriptive detail, merge repetitions, and expose blank paper. Lower abstraction when recognition requires it; raise it only when requested or the scene remains unmistakable.

Choose one primary grammar and at most one supporting grammar:
- silhouette for figures, trees, roofs or strong profiles;
- broken contour for architecture, paths and railings;
- sparse ink/halftone field for atmosphere;
- interrupted rhythm for windows, waves, posts and steps;
- cut-paper masses for strong organic or geometric shapes.

Use one main print treatment—selective woodcut, photocopy/xerox, halftone, risograph, letterpress or cut-paper—rather than combining every process.

For each illustrated section decide what to retain, merge, omit, transform and leave blank. Do not trace a full scene or apply vintage texture to a literal copy.

Dense foliage and micro-texture require stronger compression: omit about 85–95% of individual leaves, needles, fine twigs and repeated organic marks; retain one main mass, 1–3 directional gestures, at most two secondary clusters, and one sparse texture sample. Preserve the source-specific lean, opening or light gap. If foliage reads as lace, coral, filigree or dense engraving at thumbnail size, merge forms instead of merely fading them.

Distinguish field extent from printed density:
- illustration may influence about 45–70% of the poster, with 55–75% of that field quiet;
- simple scenes may keep 65–85% of the field quiet;
- intricate/foliage scenes: 65–85% quiet field, about 10–25% active illustration across the poster;
- other large fields: about 15–35% active illustration.
Use one dominant mass, 1–2 supporting marks, one restrained texture field, and at most two neutral ink values besides paper and the added hue. Enlarge forms before adding detail.

## Source-driven composition

Default to vertical 3:5 unless overridden. Use warm cream matte paper, flat orthographic scan, diffuse light, subtle fibers/grain and restrained weathering. Avoid mockup framing and artificial depth.

Choose by source geometry and visual weight, not habit:
- transformative seam: about 30–50% photography; continue a real subject, path or horizon into illustration;
- underprint overlay: about 35–60% photography with broad low-density illustration/color behind selected areas;
- photo anchor with expansive field: about 25–45% photography and 45–70% illustration field;
- directional split: start near 55/45–65/35 and align with a real scene axis;
- irregular fragments: about 25–50% combined photography; the largest fragment preserves the core subject.

These are starting ranges, not crop quotas. Keep figure–ground clarity, dominant/subordinate weight, breathing room in front of gaze or movement, an intentional eye path, and internal negative space. Do not default to centered photo plus caption.

## Hand-torn boundary

The main photo-to-paper handoff must have a visibly hand-torn fibrous edge, not only digital dissolve. Use irregular notches/scallops, occasional longer fibers, exposed warm paper and selective abrasion; vary nearly straight and ragged segments.

Keep the fibrous band roughly 1–4% of the short edge, readable along about 35–70% of the visible photographic perimeter. These guides are subordinate to subject integrity. Let selected illustrated/color forms pass behind, cross or emerge from torn segments without hiding the tear.

At 1–2 meaningful pressure points only, allow subordinate speckles, halftone crumbs or 1–3 faint incomplete ghost marks. Keep scale/spacing irregular, colors within the paper/neutral/single-hue system, and the quiet field genuinely blank. No uniform deckled frame, sticker outline, crisp rectangular clipping, heavy shadow, curled corners or thick layered-paper depth.

## Structural color

Use exactly one added print hue; natural photographic colors do not count as added hues. Choose a saturated analogous hue, focused complement, temperature bridge or meaningful minor source color. Specify its exact hue and material; do not weaken it with pale/pastel/muted wording unless requested.

Choose one integration mode:
- continue a source contour beyond the photo;
- selectively replace a real region while protecting defining subject detail;
- underprint behind and partly through photo and illustration;
- derive a positive/negative counterform from a source shape;
- extend a source-derived directional rhythm.

Give color one primary function: focal reinforcement, counterweight, bridge, eye direction, figure–ground clarity or semantic emphasis. It must satisfy at least two tests: source-derived shape/position/rhythm; physical contact with image forms; crossing/transformation at the boundary; changed eye path/balance; strengthened source meaning. Removing it should weaken composition or interpretation; otherwise redesign it.

Area guides: opaque replacement/cut-paper 2–6%; translucent/halftone underprint 6–15%; large structural field 10–20%. Use less with an already vivid source; with a large field reduce opacity, neutral density and competing detail. At most 1–2 subordinate source-anchored echoes. Grain, ink bite and misregistration must not turn the hue neon/glossy or wash it out. No detached corner patch, arbitrary dot, swatch or generic shape unrelated to the source.

## Micro-text

Use supplied wording exactly, without unsolicited rewriting, subtitle or attribution. Otherwise author one scene-derived emotional trace:
- English-only by default, at most 5 words: one word, 2–4 keywords, or a short phrase;
- Chinese-only when requested, at most 8 Han characters;
- bilingual when requested, with each limit applied independently and one line clearly subordinate.

Length limits govern authored copy, not an instruction to truncate supplied wording. Use one separator system for keywords. Do not infer language from the location/mood or add dates, coordinates, serials, labels, fake quotations or attribution unless requested/source-essential.

Keep type subordinate: approximately 1.3–2.8% of poster height for English, 1.5–3.5% for Chinese. Prefer vintage typewriter serif/monospaced letterpress, faint handwriting/pencil or restrained Song-style print. Use one paper-integrated treatment, readable spelling, uneven ink pressure/absorbency and quiet charcoal/graphite/brown-black or the existing hue. Avoid polished display lettering and bold all-caps.

Place text in genuine quiet paper, aligned to a source/torn edge or visual axis, never across a face or defining detail. One line by default; two related lines for bilingual copy. Text is the final resting point, not a headline. Add no decorative rule or icon just to support it.

## Rendering and acceptance

Use the built-in image editing capability with the actual source; consult the imagegen skill for current tool mechanics. Send only necessary references/prompt and do not upload private sources elsewhere. Original/result comparison is a separate confirmed output, not this style's internal collage.

Compile four compact paragraphs: canvas/attention geometry; photographic invariants; abstraction/color/torn edge/exact text; print mood and relevant exclusions. Include visible decisions, not file paths or design-theory explanations.

Inspect the actual result at normal and thumbnail scale:
- complete recognizable subjects and truthful photography;
- large, genuinely simplified illustration with quiet internal space;
- source-derived shared geometry and structurally useful single hue;
- visible irregular fibers without noisy border or digital mask;
- exact, legible, subordinate micro-text and coherent eye path.
Correct the observed failure once, preserving successful regions; if it still fails, report the limitation rather than claiming success.

Reject literal tracing, dense botanical detail, filler symbols, arbitrary accents, multiple added hues/illustration grammars, scrapbooking, repeated dotted borders, generic AI scenery, waxy retouching, over-sharpening, commercial hierarchy, logos, CTA, watermark, glossy/3D/neon/cartoon treatment.

Export at least 2K long edge without stretching. Disclose non-generative enlargement rather than claiming recovered native detail. Preserve originals/prior outputs and basenames in separate result folders; no unsolicited suffixes. Return the image/path and 1–3 concise sentences of rationale. Show the full prompt or detailed notes only when asked; no automatic promotion or generation-count notices.
